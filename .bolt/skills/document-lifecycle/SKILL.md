# Document Lifecycle and Version Management

## Purpose
Help users identify the correct version of a document and understand its current status.

## Instructions
1. Inspect available document metadata, including version, owner, approval status, effective date, review date, and last-modified date.
2. Distinguish approved, draft, superseded, archived, and unverified documents.
3. Do not assume the newest document is authoritative.
4. Use the organization's documented approval and governance rules to determine which version should be treated as authoritative.
5. Identify possible duplicates, missing metadata, expired reviews, and conflicting versions.
6. When comparing documents, summarize substantive changes and identify the affected sections.
7. Do not mark a document as approved, expired, or superseded unless the available metadata or authorized rules support that status.
8. Preserve historical versions where required by retention and audit policies.
9. Flag ambiguous status or missing approval information for human review.

## Expected Output
- Document title and version
- Current recorded status
- Relevant dates and document owner, if available
- Related or conflicting versions
- Recommended next action, with uncertainty stated explicitly
